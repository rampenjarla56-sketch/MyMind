# MyMind — Competitive Research (Sleep, Focus & Screen-Time Apps, 2025–2026)

Research date: October 2026 (live web search). Pricing and store ratings change often; figures below are the most recent found and are marked where sources conflict.

## What this means for MyMind

- **Nobody owns the teen "bedtime drifted to 5 a.m." use case.** No mainstream app is built for gradually shifting a delayed sleep phase earlier; the closest (DOZE, a Canadian research app for high-schoolers, and Shifti, a small 15-minute-step shifter) are obscure or adult-oriented [26][27]. Clinical guidance (shift bedtime 15–30 min earlier every few nights, consistent wake time, morning light) exists but is unproductized [24][25] — this is exactly MyMind's core loop.
- **Pricing is a moat for a free app.** The incumbents cluster at $40–100/yr: Sleep Cycle ~$39.99/yr [1][2], RISE $69.99/yr [6], Calm $69.99–79.99/yr [10][11], BetterSleep $59.99/yr [14], Alarmy ~$59.99/yr [18], Opal $99.99/yr [20], Forest Plus ~$59.99/yr [23]. A genuinely free, no-ads tool is differentiated by itself.
- **Billing/trial complaints are the #1 recurring review theme** across RISE, Calm, Alarmy, Opal and BetterSleep (surprise trial-to-paid charges, hard cancellation) [7][12][13][16][20]. "No accounts, no subscription, data on-device" directly answers the loudest pain point in the category.
- **Engagement mechanics conflict with sleep, and users notice.** Pokémon Sleep's grind "adds an undercurrent of stress" and punishes bad nights by withholding rewards [31][32]; Tom's Guide argued making sleep "exciting" is self-defeating [33]. MyMind's no-streak-shame, miss-never-resets principle is a real differentiator, not just ethics.
- **Dense dashboards go unused.** Sleep Cycle reviewers note detailed stats stop being looked at after the novelty fades [2]; RISE users call the data "overwhelming" and "difficult to interpret" [7][8]. One-action-per-screen and a simple morning log fit how people actually behave.
- **Tracking accuracy is a chronic complaint** (Sleep Cycle false sleep detection [3], Pokémon Sleep mis-timed nights [30]). MyMind sidesteps this entirely by using a self-reported morning log instead of sensing.
- **Wind-down content is table stakes but almost always paywalled** (Calm, BetterSleep, Sleep Cycle premium libraries). A free built-in 5-step routine (brain dump, 4-7-8, body scan) covers the same job without a content library.
- **The "friction at night" idea is validated by one sec/Opal** — a brief pause measurably cuts app opens (one sec claims ~57% fewer opens after a week) [28][29]. MyMind's "phone away" step and dark "I can't sleep" screen apply the same principle at bedtime rather than all day.
- **Smart alarms are the incumbents' hero feature, not MyMind's.** Sleep Cycle/Alarmy own waking; MyMind's edge is the *falling-asleep-earlier* side, which none of them seriously address.
- **Teen-specific risk note:** a 2025 randomized trial found short (4-week) interventions did not reliably shift adolescent circadian phase [34], and DSP is often stable into adulthood [35] — MyMind should set expectations of slow progress and avoid medical claims (which it already does).

## Sleep Cycle

- **Features:** Smart alarm that wakes you in light sleep (the headline feature); sound-based sleep tracking; free tier has the alarm, basic tracking and 14-night history; Premium adds long-term trends, sleep notes, snore recording/analysis, a sleep-aid content library, data export, and a newer AI coach ("Luma") [1][2].
- **Pricing:** Free tier + Premium. Sources conflict: ~$39.99/yr is the most-cited current figure, with offers from $2.99–57.99 shown in-store; Sleep Cycle publishes no price on its own site and trial lengths are advertised inconsistently (7 vs 60 days) [1][2][4].
- **Praise:** Smart alarm genuinely liked; solid ratings (~4.6 App Store / 4.5 Play) [1].
- **Complaints:** Accuracy (marked asleep while awake; alarm fired at 3:30 a.m. instead of 6:30) [3]; feature stagnation vs competitors; nightly recording failures on Android; aggressive premium upsell; detailed stats that users stop reviewing after the novelty wears off [2][3].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** None. It optimizes wake time, not bedtime shifting.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** Don't compete on sensing/smart alarms. Its 14-day free history shows even "free" tiers gate data — MyMind's fully-local log is cleaner.

## RISE (Rise Science)

- **Features:** Sleep-need estimate + rolling 14-night "sleep debt" tracker; circadian energy-peak predictions ("energy schedule"); 20+ sleep-hygiene reminders timed to the body clock; guided breathing/relaxation, sleep sounds; phone-only tracking via "tappigraphy" (no wearable needed); smart alarm; wearable/calendar sync [5][6].
- **Pricing:** Hard paywall after a 7-day trial; $69.99/yr (monthly and lifetime options exist; older reviews cite ~$60/yr) [5][6][9].
- **Praise:** The sleep-debt number is motivating and legible ("showed I was 8–10 hours behind each week"); 4.7 stars from 37K+ App Store reviews per Bustle [8][9].
- **Complaints:** Trial-to-paid billing surprises and refund difficulty are the top theme; tappigraphy breaks on irregular schedules (logged sleep while the user was working, manual correction sometimes blocked); data overload/confusing UI; pushy notifications; generic advice [7][8].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** Closest conceptually — it's circadian-aware and nudges schedule consistency — but it's an adult productivity/energy app, $70/yr, and not designed to walk a 5 a.m. bedtime back to midnight.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** Validates circadian framing and timed bedtime nudges; its failures (paywall, data overload, fragile auto-tracking) are the exact things MyMind avoids.

## Calm

- **Features:** Meditation + sleep content giant: 100+ guided meditations, weekly-updated Sleep Stories (celebrity-narrated), sleep music/soundscapes, breathing exercises, Masterclasses; launched a standalone "Calm Sleep" iOS app in Sept 2025 [10][11][36].
- **Pricing:** Very limited free tier (one breathing exercise, day-1 of programs, select stories). Premium $69.99–79.99/yr or $16.99/mo (sources vary); family $99.99/yr; lifetime $299.99–499.99 (varies); Calm Sleep is $69.99/yr standalone [10][11][36].
- **Praise:** Sleep Stories are consistently cited as the single feature that justifies the subscription; production quality of content [11][12].
- **Complaints:** Billing/cancellation dominates Trustpilot (unauthorized renewals, charged during "free" trial, slow support, no phone contact); $80/yr seen as poor value; 7-day trial too short; cluttered interface; new content added more slowly than promised [12][13].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** None specific; it's general-audience relaxation content.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** Wind-down content works but is paywalled and engagement-heavy (big bright library UI). MyMind's built-in brain dump + 4-7-8 + body relaxation delivers the "help me switch off" job with zero content costs and a dark one-task UI.

## BetterSleep (ex-Relax Melodies)

- **Features:** Its strength is the sound mixer — layer 300+ sounds/music (white noise, nature, ASMR) into custom mixes; 250+ meditations/hypnosis sessions, 100+ sleep stories; basic sleep tracker + recorder, snore detection, smart alarm [14][15].
- **Pricing:** Free download but most of the library moved behind Premium (~$9.99/mo or $59.99/yr, 7-day trial; lifetime ~$249 appears periodically; one aggregator shows a $99.99 IAP — conflicting) [14][15][16].
- **Praise:** Best-in-class sound mixing and sleep-onset help; high store ratings (~4.7, 390K ratings per aggregators) [14][15].
- **Complaints:** The 2024–25 shift to a paid model angered long-time users (sounds they'd used for years got paywalled; developer publicly acknowledged the move) [16]; tracking rated shallow (2.5/5 in one review); price seen as high for a sound app; loading-screen/stability issues [15][16].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** None.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** Shows backlash risk when "free" becomes bait. If MyMind ever adds sounds, a few good free loops beat a giant paywalled library.

## Alarmy

- **Features:** "Mission alarm" — the alarm only stops after a task: math, barcode/QR scan, photo of a location, typing, memory game, shaking, squats; multiple missions per alarm (added Sept 2025); loud/escalating sounds; wake-up check; also sleep sounds, sleep analysis, snore detection; video alarms [17][18][19].
- **Pricing:** Free with ads + 4 free missions (sources disagree which four); Premium ~$5/mo or $59.99/yr with auto-converting 7-day trial [18].
- **Praise:** Extremely effective for heavy sleepers — the mission mechanic genuinely forces you out of bed; 4.7–4.8 stars across listings [17][19].
- **Complaints:** Battery drain; occasional alarm failures (silent or wrong sound); creeping ads/paywalling of basics; wake-up-check nags that won't stay off; subscription/billing issues; heavy Android permissions (device-admin to prevent uninstall, accessibility API) [17][19].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** None, but very popular with students for forcing wake-ups.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** The mission idea could inspire a *gentle* morning log prompt ("get up, log your night"), but Alarmy is adversarial by design — the opposite of MyMind's tone. Its reliability complaints show alarms are high-stakes; MyMind should keep its reminders low-stakes.

## Opal

- **Features:** iOS/Mac app & website blocker on schedules or on demand; Deep Focus mode that can't be quit early (survives app deletion); pickup/usage analytics beyond Apple Screen Time; gamification with gems, streaks, leaderboards; daily "Opal Score" [20][21].
- **Pricing:** Restrictive free tier (one recurring session, today-only score — "feels more like a trial"); Pro $19.99/mo, $99.99/yr, $399 lifetime; up to 50% student discount [20][21].
- **Praise:** Beautiful, premium design; Deep Focus creates real accountability; on-device data processing via Apple's Screen Time API [20][21].
- **Complaints:** Price is the dominant complaint ("$100/yr for what iOS does free"); hard onboarding upsell; weaker Android version; free tier too thin [20][21].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** Marketed partly at parents/students (Common Sense Media reviews it), but no sleep-schedule features [22].
- **Relevance to MyMind:** Proof that people will pay to be protected from their phone at night — and that a $100 price excludes teens. MyMind's "phone away" step delivers the nightly slice of this for free, without OS-level blocking complexity.

## one sec

- **Features:** Friction, not blocking: intercepts flagged app launches and forces a short breathing pause, then asks if you still want to open it; attempt counter per app per day; custom interventions and journaling; doesn't cover browser visits [28][29].
- **Pricing:** Free for one app; premium figures conflict across sources ($19.99/yr, $3.99–4.99/mo, ~$39.99–50/yr, $99.99 lifetime — region/date dependent) [28][29].
- **Praise:** It works for light-to-moderate scrollers — a PNAS-cited study and the company claim ~57% fewer app opens after a week; 4.7–4.8 stars at scale; the attempt counter is an eye-opener [28][29].
- **Complaints:** Determined users just breathe through it and open the app anyway; same intervention for every app; unpleasant vibration; premium price for a single trick [28][29].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** None explicitly, though the mechanic suits late-night doomscrolling.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** Strong evidence that a calm pause + an intention question changes behavior — a good pattern for MyMind's "I can't sleep" button (breathe first, then one gentle option) instead of punitive blocking.

## Forest

- **Features:** Pomodoro-style focus timer that grows a virtual tree which dies if you leave the app; coins buy new trees and fund real tree planting (Trees for the Future); allow-lists, stats, achievements, friend forests, Chrome site blocker; soft nudge, not a hard block [23].
- **Pricing:** Historically a $3.99 one-time purchase; moved in Dec 2025 to free download + "Forest Plus" subscription (~$59.99/yr, region-dependent), with ads outside sessions on free; some trees still cost extra coins even for payers [23].
- **Praise:** Charming, low-friction gamification; the real-tree planting hook; stopwatch mode and plant variety [23].
- **Complaints:** Crashes/freezes on both platforms; anger at previously-purchased features (tags) moving behind the subscription; gamification fatigue; thin insights vs built-in Screen Time; easy to bypass [23].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** Popular with students for study focus, but nothing sleep-related.
- **Relevance to MyMind:** The loved part of Forest is positive, loss-free-ish symbolism. If MyMind ever visualizes progress, "something grows when you wind down, and never dies when you miss" borrows the warmth while honoring no-streak-shame. Its paid-to-subscription backlash is another cautionary tale.

## Pokémon Sleep

- **Features:** Gamified sleep tracker: phone on mattress (or Pokémon GO Plus+ band) tracks sleep; sleep quality/duration feeds raising Snorlax and collecting Pokémon "sleep styles"; events, research areas (v3.0.0 added Amber Canyon, Oct 2025), diary (premium) [30][31][37].
- **Pricing:** Free with aggressive monetization: Premium Pass $9.99/mo or $49.99/6-months (14-day first trial; unlocks full sleep history vs 30 days, bonus points, premium items); diamond bundles $1.19 up to ~$98 [31][38].
- **Praise:** Charming, genuinely motivates consistent bedtimes for Pokémon fans; Metacritic ~70 [32].
- **Complaints:** Tracking unreliability (overestimated sleep-onset, wrong-day attribution for early-morning bedtimes — directly relevant to a 5 a.m. sleeper); punishes bad nights by withholding rewards/energy; grind and "undercurrent of stress" from the paid pass; battery drain with screen-on tracking; mic picks up partners; aggressive microtransactions [30][31][32].
- **Teen/phase-shift angle:** Appeals to teens by aesthetic, but a late sleeper gets *penalized* (wrong-day logging, missed rewards), and Tom's Guide argued making sleep a game to win is counterproductive [33].
- **Relevance to MyMind:** The strongest proof that engagement-first sleep design backfires. MyMind's "a miss never resets progress" is the direct answer to this failure mode.

## Content sources

Verified October 2026 (live web search). These back the specific techniques used in the app's copy (wind-down steps, body-relaxation script, "can't sleep" triage, morning alarm text). None of this makes the app medical advice; the in-app disclaimer stays.

- **Teens need 8–10 hours** — the app's wake-time math assumes this range. AASM consensus recommendation for ages 13–18: [AASM teen sleep duration recommendation (PDF)](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/teen-sleep-duration-recommendation.pdf).
- **Fixed wake time, even weekends** — keep bed and wake times consistent, weekend lie-ins within ~2 hours: [AASM — Helping teens get healthy sleep: five tips for parents](https://aasm.org/helping-teens-get-healthy-sleep-five-tips-for-parents/); [American Thoracic Society — Healthy Sleep in Teens (PDF)](https://member.thoracic.org/patients/patient-resources/resources/healthy-sleep-in-teens.pdf).
- **Gradual bedtime shift, not a heroic jump** — AASM's back-to-school guidance is to move bed and wake times earlier in ~15-minute nightly steps: [AASM — Sleep is key to help teens recharge for the school year](https://aasm.org/sleep-is-key-to-help-teens-recharge-for-success-this-school-year/). For delayed sleep phase specifically, gradual earlier shifts plus a steady wake time: [KidsHealth (Nemours) — Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder in Teens](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/delayed-sleep-teens.html). (The app defaults to 30 min/night with a 15-min "gentle" option.)
- **Stimulus control / get up if awake ~20 min** — the "I can't sleep" screen (get up, dim light, something boring, return when sleepy; bed is for sleep) is standard CBT-I stimulus control: [Cleveland Clinic — Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/cognitive-behavioral-therapy-insomnia).
- **4-7-8 / slow breathing** — inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8, practiced for a few rounds before bed: [Sleep Foundation — Relaxation exercises to help fall asleep](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/relaxation-exercises-to-help-fall-asleep).
- **Progressive relaxation / body scan** — the relaxParts script (release muscle groups one by one, end on the breath) follows PMR and body-scan practice: [Sleep Foundation — Relaxation exercises](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/relaxation-exercises).
- **Brain dump / writing down worries** — a quiet wind-down routine that offloads the day's thoughts before bed is standard NHS adolescent advice: [NHS (ELFT) — Sleep advice for adolescents (PDF)](https://elft.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/2022-06/sleep_advice_for_adolescents.pdf); [NHS Healthier Together — Sleep hacks for teenagers (PDF)](https://cdn.healthiertogether.nhs.uk/docs/Sleep_hacks_for_teenagers.pdf).
- **Morning light within ~30 min of waking** — daylight soon after waking is the main cue that pulls the body clock earlier (hence the alarm's "open the curtains, type UP" task): [AASM — five tips for parents](https://aasm.org/helping-teens-get-healthy-sleep-five-tips-for-parents/) (open curtains; daylight is a timing cue); [KidsHealth — Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder in Teens](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/delayed-sleep-teens.html).
- **Caffeine cutoff (~2 p.m. / early afternoon)** — guidance ranges from "none after school" (AASM, above) to none within 4–6 hours of bed: [NHS Hillingdon CYP — Sleeping (11–19)](https://www.hillingdoncyp.cnwl.nhs.uk/11-19-years/lifestyle-choices/sleeping); [American Thoracic Society — Healthy Sleep in Teens (PDF)](https://member.thoracic.org/patients/patient-resources/resources/healthy-sleep-in-teens.pdf). The app's ~2 p.m. rule is the conservative end of that range.
- **Screens off at wind-down, phone out of reach** — devices out of the bedroom at night and a screen-free window before bed: [NHS Healthier Together — Sleep hacks for teenagers (PDF)](https://cdn.healthiertogether.nhs.uk/docs/Sleep_hacks_for_teenagers.pdf).
- **"If it lasts weeks, talk to someone"** — persistent sleep trouble or low mood is a see-a-professional flag, which the app states on the home screen: [KidsHealth — Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder in Teens](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/delayed-sleep-teens.html); [Cleveland Clinic — CBT-I](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/cognitive-behavioral-therapy-insomnia).
