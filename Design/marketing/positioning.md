# Positioning

Draft for owner review. Nothing here is final and nothing gets published without approval.

## The one-sentence positioning statement

> For teens whose bedtime has drifted to 5 or 6 a.m., MyMind is a free web app with no account and no ads that helps build a consistent bedtime by moving it a little earlier each night, with a 30-minute wind-down that gets you off the feed.

(If we rename, swap the name. The sentence holds.)

## Name and tagline options

A real issue first: "MyMind" collides with mymind.com, an established paid bookmarking/notes app with the same name. Search for "MyMind app" and we lose. That alone is a reason to consider renaming before any marketing spend of effort. Counterpoint: the URL and PWA install name already say MyMind, and renaming costs a new URL (or a redirect) plus re-installs for the few current users. Cheap to do now, expensive later.

| # | Name | Tagline | Notes |
|---|------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **MyMind** (keep) | "One bedtime. Tonight." | Zero migration cost. But generic, says nothing about sleep, and the name is taken by a known app. |
| 2 | **Earlier** | "A little earlier every night." | Names the exact mechanism. Honest, calm, easy to say in a video. Top recommendation. |
| 3 | **Backshift** | "Walk your bedtime back." | Describes the core loop literally. Slight work-shift connotation. |
| 4 | **Wind Down** | "30 minutes between you and sleep." | Names the nightly ritual rather than the goal. Common phrase, hard to own in search. |
| 5 | **Midnight** | "Get back to midnight." | The destination as the name. Memorable, but the app targets 12 to 1 a.m., not midnight sharp. |
| 6 | **Tonight** | "One clear bedtime. Tonight." | Matches the Vision promise word for word. Very generic as a search term. |
| 7 | **Settle** | "Settle in. Switch off." | Calm, fits the dark UI. Doesn't say what the app does. |
| 8 | **Dusk** | "Dim the night, not just the screen." | Pretty, on-tone. Several existing apps named Dusk. |
| 9 | **Unscroll** | "It's 1 a.m. and you're not scrolling." | Names the enemy. Great for short video. Slightly negative framing for an app that's mostly about routine. |
| 10 | **5AM Club (inverted): "Was 5AM"** | "I used to go to bed at 5 a.m." | Story-as-name. Funny, teen-coded, but ages badly once the story isn't yours alone. |

**Recommendation:** rename to **Earlier**, tagline **"A little earlier every night."** It is the product in four words, it makes no promise beyond the mechanism, it's searchable, and it reads naturally in a teen's mouth on camera ("I built an app called Earlier"). If the owner wants zero migration cost, keep MyMind and use "One bedtime. Tonight." as the tagline, but accept the name collision.

## Three key messages

Grounded in DesignNotes/Research.md. Every message stays inside the allowed claim: the app *helps build a consistent bedtime*. No medical claims, no "fix your sleep", no "cure".

### 1. Actually free. Not trial-free.

The incumbents cluster at $40 to $100 a year: Sleep Cycle ~$40, RISE and Calm ~$70, Opal $100. The single loudest complaint in their reviews is billing: surprise trial-to-paid charges and cancellations that take effort. We have no price, no account, no trial, no ads, and nothing to cancel, because there is nothing to sign up for. Data stays on your phone.

Use in copy: "Free. No account. No ads. Nothing to cancel because you never signed up."
Never say: "free forever" (we can't promise forever), "better than [named app]" in paid-comparison form without the owner's sign-off.

### 2. Miss a night, lose nothing.

Sleep apps that gamify bedtime punish bad nights. Pokémon Sleep withholds rewards when you sleep late; streak apps make one slip feel like starting over. This app has no streaks, no scores, no shame. A missed night just resets tonight's target. That's a design principle, not a missing feature.

Use in copy: "No streaks. No guilt. A bad night just means tonight's target moves, that's all."
Never say: anything implying users of other apps are weak or failing, or any guilt-based hook aimed back at our own users.

### 3. Built by a teen who was going to bed at 6 a.m.

Nobody owns the "my bedtime drifted to 5 a.m." problem. The big apps optimize waking up or sell relaxation content; none of them walk a 5 a.m. bedtime back to 12. The builder is a teenager who had exactly this problem and built the tool he needed, then used it himself. Progress is gradual by design, 15 to 30 minutes at a time, because jumping from 5:30 to 12:30 in one night doesn't work.

Use in copy: "I built this for myself first. If it didn't help me sleep, it wasn't done."
Never say: "proven", "clinically", "guaranteed", or any before/after framed as a medical result. The before/after is a personal story of bedtimes, shown from the app's own log.

## Claim guardrails (applies to every asset)

- Allowed: "helps build a consistent bedtime", "a wind-down that takes you off the feed", "moves your bedtime earlier a little each night".
- Not allowed: cure, treat, fix (as in "fixes insomnia"), insomnia claims, DSPD treatment claims, "doctor-approved", sleep-quality or health-outcome promises.
- The personal story is told as a story ("here's what happened to me"), never as a predicted result ("this will happen to you").
- Audience includes under-18s: no countdown timers, no "only today", no fake scarcity, no FOMO hooks. The app is free and will be there tomorrow, and the copy should sound like it.
